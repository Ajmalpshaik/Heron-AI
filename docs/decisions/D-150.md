### Context

By 2026-11-07 the Heron tab carried seven buttons: Heron, four padlocks (Changes, Admin, Publish, Ask to
Keep), Free this model and Companion. The owner asked for a tidier ribbon: the switches in one settings
window, each one's state being its own button, Free this model in the Heron button's menu like Bridge
Status, and short, plain hover texts.

### Decision

1. **The ribbon keeps three things.** The **Heron** button, whose menu holds **Bridge Status** and **Free
   this model** - both as text with no picture; a **Settings** button with a gear in the brand's colours;
   and **Companion**, as it was.
2. **The four switches live in the Settings window:** Changes, Admin, Publish and Ask to Keep, in that
   order. Each row has its padlock picture, its name, one short line, and **its state as the button** -
   ON lit (Error for a padlock, Success for Ask to Keep, D-149), OFF quiet. A line under the card says
   Admin and Publish work only while Changes is on.
3. **Pressing a state runs the switch's own command**, unchanged: the same question on the way to the
   risky side (on for the padlocks, off for Ask to Keep), the same plain fallback when a window cannot
   draw, the same setting in `heron.config`. The window is modal, so the toggle runs on Revit's thread,
   inside `SettingsCommand`, as a ribbon press did. Every row is read again after each press.
4. **Hover texts are one short sentence each** on every Heron button.
5. **Every sentence that sent the modeller to a ribbon padlock now sends them to Settings** - the refusal
   a chat reads when a switch is off, the Bridge Status window, the questions' plain fallbacks.

### Consequences

- **Easy:** a ribbon of three buttons; one place for every switch, with the state as the thing to click.
- **Hard:** **the ribbon no longer shows at a glance whether Changes is on.** The padlock that answered
  "can Heron change this model right now?" without a click is inside the window. The owner chose it,
  told so before choosing. The refusal still names the switch when a write is refused, and the Bridge
  Status window still shows Changes. Putting one indicator back on the ribbon would be a small change.
- **Pending checks** that say to press a switch on the ribbon mean the same switch in Settings;
  [NEEDS-CHECKING](../NEEDS-CHECKING.md) says so once, at the top.
- **Not decided here:** the activity banner, which follows the designer's status design in its own change.

### Amended 2026-11-08 — the Companion's switch joins the others, and its button has no arrow

**Accepted:** Ajmal PS, 2026-11-08, in conversation: *"do it what ever balance"* - the balance item he had
been shown as *"Companion: on/off switch into Settings, and a push button"*.

Point 1 kept **Companion, as it was**: a split button whose arrow held *Turn Companion off / on*
([D-109](D-109.md)). That arrow was the last switch left on the ribbon. Now:

1. **The Companion's on/off is the fifth row in Settings**, after Ask to Keep, drawn like the others: its
   picture (orange on, grey off), its name, one short line, and its state as the button. Its ON is lit in
   Success, as Ask to Keep's is - it only shows a page and never changes the model, so it is not a state
   to warn about. Pressing it runs `CompanionToggleCommand`, unchanged, and the row is read from the
   shared setting (`companion.enabled`), not this Revit's cached copy.
2. **The ribbon's Companion is one push button.** It opens the page; its picture still says on or off.
   Pressed while off, it says so and names the way back: *Heron tab > Settings > Companion*.
3. **Every sentence that sent the modeller to "the arrow under the Companion button"** - the button's own
   message and the six the chat reads when the Companion is off - now sends them to Settings.

What the switch does is [D-109](D-109.md)'s, unchanged. Pending checks that say to use the arrow under
Companion ([Group BK](../needs-checking/group-bk.md) BK8) mean the same switch in Settings.
