### Context

The cross-encoder read a card's semantic identity, capability and domain, and nothing else. Its own code
said why - the utterances live in the card's file, and reading the library for every question costs
seconds - and what to do if that ever mattered: *"if a measurement ever shows the utterances change a
re-ranked order, the fix is to carry them into the store, not to load the library per query."*

Rows [5b-467](../fragment-issues/section-5b-rows-401-425.md), 5b-483, 5b-484, 5b-485 and 5b-510 measured it,
each on the owner's backend (the trained model with the re-ranker): a sentence declared on a card reached
it by identity, and its paraphrases went to other cards. Each time the fix was to reword the card's
identity on the owner's word - `COPY_ELEMENTS`, `REPORT_SPOT_DIMENSIONS`, `OFFSET_TAGS_FROM_HOST`, and
`RENAME_ELEMENTS`, a PROVEN card, in row 5b-510.

### Decision

1. **A fragment's re-ranker passage is its identity, then its first declared utterances - at most
   `PASSAGE_UTTERANCES` and `PASSAGE_WORDS` of them, whole sentences only - then its capability and
   domain.** Eight and 64 when this was decided.
2. **The utterances are carried in the knowledge store**, in `rerank_said`, written by
   `heron_search.index` in the pass that already reads every card. A question reads them from the store
   for its shortlist only, and never opens a card file. `INDEX_FORMAT` 3 rebuilds every store's search
   index once.
3. **A store indexed before `rerank_said` existed still answers**, every passage the identity, capability
   and domain it was. Any other database fault raises.
4. **Only the re-ranker reads them.** The words route, the nearness route, identity and the cache are
   unchanged, so on a machine without a re-ranker every answer is the one it was.

### Alternatives considered

- **Leave the passage alone and reword identities, row by row.** What rows 5b-467 to 5b-510 did. Each
  reword of a PROVEN card needs the owner, and each bought back some paraphrases and left others.
- **Three utterances, not eight.** Measured on the same frozen sentences: no question set rose on any
  backend and his 79 gained as much (first place 12 to 17, 12 to 19 on lexical with the re-ranker), but
  **no paraphrase gained** (37 to 37, 39 to 38) - the problem the rows were about.
- **Five.** Measured on `db313f4`: two question sets rose by one on the owner's backend, and paraphrases
  gained only two.
- **Read the utterances from the library per question.** Rejected by the code's own rule: it is the
  whole library read to describe twenty rows.

### Consequences

- **On the owner's backend**, his 79 (through `score-routing`, on `e309c81`): first place 12 to 17, top
  three 18 to 27, a question handed a change 16 to 11. The 120 plain questions answered by a change 34
  to 24. `check-routing`'s claimed-and-not-reached 115 to 41; `check-risk-crossings --revit 2024`'s
  questions answered by a write over a read 11 to 9. Paraphrases of rows 467 to 510 reaching their card
  37 to 43 of 60.
- **Costs, recorded in row 5b-978:** the re-rank reads twice as many tokens and took about twice as long
  here (+80 ms a question on one thread); some questions newly reach a write while more leave one - the
  rows' own 39 questions 12 to 13 on the owner's backend; three `tests/test_routing_phrases.py` guards
  that passed on `main` fail, among them a question reaching an ADMIN write; two `tests/test_retrieve.py`
  checks on *"show me every duct in the model"* fail on the owner's backend.
- **Identities chosen against the identity-only re-ranker were chosen against a passage that no longer
  exists.** Rows 5b-483, 5b-484 and 5b-510 picked wordings by scoring them on it; their trades re-open,
  and row 5b-978 names the ones measured.
- **The order of a card's `utterances` now matters** on the owner's backend: the first eight are read.
