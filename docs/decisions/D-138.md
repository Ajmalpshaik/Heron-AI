### Context

D-126 put Revit's Keep or Discard box in front of every change a chat keeps, and item 5 gave the owner one
way to turn it off: `write.askBeforeKeeping` in `heron.config`, never a field in the request. The owner
runs long jobs - a family is six to ten changes - and walks away while they run. With the box on every
change, the job stopped at the first one and waited for him; with the question off, nothing at all was
asked for any job, and turning it back on meant opening a text file. On 2026-10-18 he asked for two
things in one sentence: a switch for the box *"same like this admin on off"*, and, while it is on, the box
*"in one"* - once per job, not once per change. *"That you can decide"* left the shape to the session.

### Decision

1. **A ribbon switch, Ask to Keep, beside Changes, Admin and Publish.** It writes `write.askBeforeKeeping`
   through `HeronPermissions.SetAsksBeforeKeeping`, which mirrors `SetAdminEnabled`. Its picture is the
   state - the padlock family with a question mark: blue and closed while Revit asks, red and open while
   changes are kept without asking - and its words say `Ask to Keep ON`, `Ask to Keep off`, or `Keep: rest
   of job`. **The default stays ON.** Its safe side is ON, the other way round from the three beside it, so
   it asks on the way **off**, once, through `HeronSwitchWindow` (*Stop asking* / *Keep asking*): changes
   asked for in a chat will be kept without asking, and Ctrl+Z still takes each one back as one step. On
   asks nothing. The add-in log says *"Ask to Keep switch set to ..."*, and `tools/heron-ready.py` section
   7 lists it beside the other three.
2. **While it is on, one Keep per job.** The box gets a third command link, *"Keep, and keep the rest of
   this job without asking"*. It keeps this change, exactly as Keep does, and grants keep-without-asking to
   the **same chat** - the bridge's client id, the one the lease is held by - on the **same model** - its
   CreationGUID, path and title, `RevitOperations.DocumentKey`.
3. **The grant ends** when that chat has sent no change for **10 minutes**; when a **different chat or a
   different model** asks - that change is asked, and the box says why it is back; when the modeller turns
   the switch **off** (and on again) - on the button at once, or in `heron.config` at the next change
   kept; when the grant's **model closes**; and when **Revit closes** - it is
   held in memory, never in `heron.config`.
4. **Only the modeller's click in Revit gives it** - Golden Rule 19, and D-126 item 5's reason, which
   stands: a caller may ask to keep, and only the modeller says yes. No request field is read for it. The
   client id says who the grant was given to and is part of the bridge's envelope, which an operation's
   own arguments cannot set; it never asks for one.
5. **Still asked every time, grant or not:** a `run_fragment_publish` - an export, a save, a sync, which
   Discard cannot undo - and a DRAFT fragment's try-once run (D-119). The third link is never offered on
   either, and the grant stands for the change after.
6. **Every change kept under the grant is still its own transaction group and its own undo entry,
   `Heron: <name>`.** One Ctrl+Z takes back one step, never the whole job.
7. **The reply says so.** A change kept under the grant carries `asked: false`, `keptUnderGrant: true`
   and `grantedAt`, and its verdict - whole and short - begins *"Kept WITHOUT ASKING the modeller, under
   the grant they gave in Revit at HH:MM"*. The click itself answers `modellerAnswer: "keep_rest"`,
   `kept: true`, with a sentence saying what was chosen.
8. **Revit shows it while it holds.** The Ask to Keep button reads `Keep: rest of job` on the red padlock,
   and Revit's Idling looks every 15 seconds while it does, so the label goes back once the ten minutes
   are up. The activity banner's closing line for each change says *"Kept without asking - job allowed at
   HH:MM"*.
9. **`revit_apply_move` keeps its own preview**, unchanged.

### What this does NOT change

- **[Article 7](../../HERON_CONSTITUTION.md) and [Article 10](../../HERON_CONSTITUTION.md) stand.** The grant is
  not the yes Article 7 asks for before deleting, purging, a bulk parameter write, a family reload, a
  workset change or anything touching a link; the chat still asks for that first. A publish is asked.
- **The switch lets nothing through.** Off, a change Changes allowed is kept at once, as D-126 item 5
  recorded for the key; Admin and Publish still decide their levels.
- **Proofs are not asked and give no grant.** `tools/batch-prove.py` never sends `apply`.

### What it accepts

- **A change kept under the grant is not shown first.** The modeller chose that for the job, after seeing
  one change of it; Article 9 records it as the second half of its one exception.
- **A Save As, or a new family's first save, changes the model's key,** so the grant ends there and the
  next change is asked. The safe side of a model whose name has just changed.
- **The ribbon can lag the ten minutes by up to 15 seconds.** The decision is made at each change, from
  the clock, never from the picture.
- **An answer given after the bridge stopped waiting** still gives the grant: the click is the modeller's,
  and the chat was told to ask them what they chose (D-126).
- **HH:MM is the PC's own clock.** A clock put back ends the grant rather than stretching it.
- **The Changes window's first promise stays word for word** - *"You see each change in Revit first, and
  nothing is kept until you choose Keep"* - because it has been seen in Revit (Z10). A job's later changes
  are kept unseen only after the modeller chose that in Revit, having seen one; its code comment says so.

### Alternatives considered

- **A request field naming the job.** Golden Rule 19: a caller able to say "this belongs to a job already
  allowed" holds a permission nobody gave it.
- **One transaction group for the whole job.** One Ctrl+Z would undo ten changes, and a group held open
  across chat turns blocks Revit between them.
- **A grant on a timer alone, for any chat.** A second chat would ride a grant given to the first.
- **Turning the switch off for the job and on after.** It works with item 1 alone, and it is forgotten -
  the grant ends itself.

### Consequences

- Article 9 and Golden Rule 17, the `revit_change` description and `host.chat` say this now; D-126's record
  carries a note that item 5 is superseded in part.
- Row [5b-1101](../FRAGMENT-ISSUES.md) records the change and [NEEDS-CHECKING Group HG](../NEEDS-CHECKING.md)
  holds the runs that would prove it.
- **Nothing here has run in Revit.** It compiles for 2020 to 2027; the grant's rule is run without Revit by
