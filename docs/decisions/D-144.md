# D-144 — A superseded decision says so under its own heading, where its citations land

> One record of [the decision log](../DECISIONS.md). It was given its own file on 2026-10-19 so that it
> can be read alone; **its words are unchanged, only its links were re-pointed.** Decisions are
> append-only: to change one, write a new decision that supersedes it.

**Status:** Proposed - built and checked in the change that wrote it, for the owner to accept or send back · **Date:** 2026-10-19 · **Source:** the third file-level reading of an external project, which the owner asked for on 2026-10-18 so that its useful ideas could be taken into Heron ([33 §5.1](../33-external-repository-research.md#51-affaan-mecc))
**Keeps:** the log's own rule, in its preamble - append-only, a reversal is a new entry and the original stays · [D-25](../DECISIONS.md#d-25--the-existing-libraries-are-studied-and-re-authored-never-imported) - the idea was studied in another project and re-authored here, nothing imported
**Affects:** [`check-supersessions.py`](../../tools/check-supersessions.py), [`test_check_supersessions.py`](../../tests/test_check_supersessions.py), [`check-docs.py`](../../tools/check-docs.py) (§13), [`tools/README.md`](../../tools/README.md), and six notes in this log, under D-24, D-37, D-99, D-101 and D-105

### Context

This log is append-only. A decision that is reversed keeps its words, and a new decision says, in a
`**Supersedes...**` field of its header, what it supersedes. That protects the reasoning. It does not, on
its own, protect the reader - because every link to a decision, anywhere in the repository, lands on the
**old** decision's heading, and the new decision is somewhere further down.

On 2026-10-19, of the ten supersessions an accepted decision declared, four were written at both ends and
six only at the new one. D-24, D-37, D-99 twice, D-101 and D-105 had each been superseded, in full or in
part, and nothing under their own headings said so. A reader sent to D-105 read *"the modeller works with
Heron in the chat only"* with no word that D-108 had put a Companion page beside the chat. Nothing checked
it: `check-docs.py` asks that a number is defined once, and `check-decision-titles.py` that it keeps its
title.

It was found by the third reading of an external project's documentation audit, which fails an
architecture record marked superseded that names no successor. That project writes the supersession at the
old end; this log writes it at the new end, so Heron's check starts there.

### Decision

1. **A decision in force that supersedes another, in full or in part, is noted under the old decision's
   heading in this index**: a `**Superseded by:**` or `**Superseded in part by:**` line linking the new
   decision and saying what it changed. The old decision's full record keeps every word; the index entry is
   its metadata, and it is where a citation lands.
2. **`tools/check-supersessions.py` checks it both ways.** Forward: every `**Supersedes...**` field in a
   decision in force - read from its full record and from its index entry - owes the note. Back: every
   such note links only decisions that exist and that name the old one in their own `**Supersedes...**`
   field. Exits 1 on a finding and 2 when it could not look.
3. **It runs as `check-docs.py` §13**, so it fails the gate run before every push and CI's job "The gates
   that must pass". It is not a step of its own; that job still runs twelve commands.
4. **In force is a Status that does not start with *Proposed* or *Superseded*.** A proposed decision has
   changed nothing yet, so the old entry owes its note on the day the new one is accepted. A decision that
   has itself been superseded no longer changes anything. Both are printed with their reason, so a pending
   note is seen and not lost.
5. **The six missing notes are written** under D-24, D-37, D-99 (two), D-101 and D-105, each in the words
   of the decision that superseded it.

### What this does NOT change

- **No decision's words.** The full records are untouched. The `> **SUPERSEDED BY**` banner some of them
  carry is a courtesy and is not asked for.
- **It reads links, not sentences.** It cannot tell whether a note says truly what the new decision
  changed; that is the writer's and the reviewer's job. And a `**Supersedes...**` field that links a
  decision only to name something beside it reads as superseding it: D-74 links D-73 that way, to name
  *"D-73's neighbour, the `expectProject` guard"*. D-74 is proposed, so nothing is owed today. When it is
  accepted the check will ask D-73 for a note, and the person accepting it writes what that note truly
  says.
- **A supersession written only in prose**, with no field in the header, is invisible to it.

### Consequences

`check-docs.py` fails on a supersession written at one end only. Whoever writes a `**Supersedes...**`
field writes the note under the old heading in the same change, and whoever accepts a proposed decision
that supersedes another writes it then. Measured 2026-10-19 on this checkout: 142 decisions read, this one
among them, 13 supersessions declared, 3 by a decision not in force, and no finding once the six notes
were written.
