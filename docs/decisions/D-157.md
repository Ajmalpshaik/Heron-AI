### Context

Every kept Heron change is one entry in Revit's undo list, named `Heron: <name>`, and Heron tells the
modeller Ctrl+Z takes it back. But a chat asked *"undo that"* could only repeat that sentence. Proposal F29
recorded why Heron never pressed Undo itself: Revit's Undo takes the **top** of the list, Revit has no API
that undoes a named entry, and the list is shared with the person modelling - an Undo sent "for Heron"
after they moved a wall takes their wall.

### Decision

1. **`revit_undo_last` posts Revit's own Undo command** (`PostableCommand.Undo`) - exactly Ctrl+Z, never a
   second change made to reverse the first.
2. **Only while Heron's last kept change is still the top of the list.** `HeronUndoGuard` hears, from the
   add-in's permanent DocumentChanged listener, every keep, commit, undo and redo in each model, a sync
   with central and a close:
   - a fragment job **kept** puts its entry on top;
   - **any other commit** in that model - the modeller's edit, another add-in's, or a Heron operation that
     is not a kept fragment job, such as an isolate - covers it **for good**. A later undo of the covering
     change is never counted back: what is on top then cannot be known reliably;
   - a commit **inside Heron's own job** (armed in that model) covers nothing - it is inside its group, and
     a dry run rolls back;
   - an **undo** while Heron's entry is on top takes it, whoever pressed it; a **redo** of Heron's own entry
     puts it back; any other redo covers it;
   - a **sync** clears Revit's list; a **close** forgets the model.
3. **Refused, with nothing sent, when** nothing was kept, the change is covered, already undone, already
   sent and not yet reported, synchronized, the model is not the one on screen (Revit's Undo acts on the
   model in front), the pinned model is not that one, or Revit cannot take a command now. Every refusal says
   *"Nothing was undone"* and what to do instead.
4. **One undo, once.** After it, the next entry down is Ctrl+Z's, not Heron's.
5. **The reply says Undo was SENT.** `PostCommand` runs it after the request returns, so it is never
   reported as done. Ctrl+Y redoes it.
6. **MODIFY, and Changes ON**, like any change. It is sent once (`idempotent=False`): a lost answer is
   never retried.

### What is not known

- **Nothing of it has run in Revit.** It compiles for 2020 to 2027 and every Revit member it calls exists in
  every release; the guard runs without Revit (`tests/Heron.UndoGuard.TestHost`). [NEEDS-CHECKING Group
  NU](../needs-checking/group-nu.md) holds the runs.
- **Whether Revit raises one undo event or several for an assimilated group**, and what names it carries.
  The guard is one entry deep for this reason.
- **Whether `PostCommand(Undo)` from an external event undoes the entry the guard expects**, and whether a
  temporary isolate or a view change Heron does not see as a commit sits on the list.

### Alternatives considered

- **Leave it to the modeller's Ctrl+Z** - proposal F29's option 2, and still the answer for anything past
  Heron's last change. It loses nothing; it only leaves *"undo that"* in a chat unanswered.
- **N undos, a whole session back** - F29's panic button. Every undo past the first lands on an entry the
  guard cannot vouch for.
- **Reverse the change by writing the old values back** - a second change with risks of its own and a
  second undo entry, which the host rule forbids.
