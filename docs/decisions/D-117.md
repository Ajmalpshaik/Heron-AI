# D-117 — A stop lasts until Revit restarts, and a restart clears it

> One record of [the decision log](../DECISIONS.md). Decisions are append-only: to change one, write a
> new decision that supersedes it.

**Status:** Accepted · **Date:** 2026-10-17 · **Accepted:** Ajmal PS, 2026-10-17, in conversation: *"Q-64: restart clears it, accept your recommendation"* · **Answers:** [Q-64](../open-questions/from-master-specification-part-2.md)
**Amends:** [21 §4](../21-resilience-and-operations.md) requirement 3 - *"It does not quietly resume on the next request or after a restart"* - in its last four words only
**Keeps:** [D-46](D-46.md) - no Emergency Stop button, and no second control · [D-116](D-116.md) item 7 - nothing over the bridge resumes · `HeronStop` exactly as it is
**Affects:** [`HeronStop.cs`](../../platform/Heron.Core/HeronStop.cs) (its comments only), [21 §4](../21-resilience-and-operations.md), [D-116](D-116.md)

### Context

[D-116](D-116.md) gave `HeronStop` a trigger again - the bridge's `stop` operation - and so the question of how
long a stop lasts became one a modeller meets. Two sources disagreed. 21 §4's requirement 3, from the Part 2
specification, asked for a stop that does not resume *"after a restart"*. `HeronStop`, since Step 6, says *"IT
DOES NOT PERSIST. Restarting Revit clears it"*, with its reason: a flag left on disk from a bad afternoon three
weeks ago would surface as Heron refusing to work, with no memory of why. Both were recorded as Q-64, with a
recommendation, for the owner.

### Decision

1. **A stop lasts until Revit is restarted, and a restart clears it.** It lives in memory, as it always has.
   Nothing is written to disk.
2. **A new request does not clear it**, nor a new chat, nor reconnecting the bridge, nor anything sent over
   the bridge. That half of 21 §4's requirement 3 stands unchanged.
3. **21 §4's requirement 3 is amended to match:** sticky until Revit restarts, not past it. The
   requirement's own text is left as written, with a note naming this decision, so the history of why it
   changed stays readable.
4. **`HeronStop.Message` already says this** - *"Heron stays stopped until Revit is restarted"* - and stays as
   it is.

### Why

A stop that survived a restart would need something a person could use to clear it, and nothing can: the
`stop` operation cannot resume, by design, and `EmergencyStopCommand` - the only caller of `Resume()` - has been
on no ribbon since D-46, where the owner chose not to have a second control. Without one, a modeller would be
editing a file to make Heron work again. Restarting Revit is a deliberate act at the machine, and the person
knows they did it.

### What it costs

A Revit that crashes while stopped, or is restarted for any other reason, comes back working. That is the
21 §4 protection given up, knowingly: the stop is for a situation happening now, and the restart is the clear
reset.
