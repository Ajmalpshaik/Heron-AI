### Context

[Article 13](../../HERON_CONSTITUTION.md), *Do not bypass security*, binding since [D-43](../decisions/D-43.md), says:
*"Request operations through the permission layer. Never call around it, never cache a permission decision,
never treat one confirmation as covering a later action."*

[D-138](../decisions/D-138.md), accepted 2026-10-18, gave the Keep or Discard box a third link, *"Keep, and keep
the rest of this job without asking"*: one click keeps that change and lets the same chat's next changes to
the same model be kept without the box, until ten minutes pass with none, another chat or model asks, the
switch is turned off, or the model or Revit closes. [D-141](../decisions/D-141.md) keys the grant on the request's
conversation. D-138 amended Article 9 to name the grant and left Article 13's words as they were, so the two
Articles read against each other: one click covers later actions, which Article 13 says never happens.
[Q-73](../open-questions/tier-2.md) set the two side by side, clause by clause, and
[D-139](../decisions/D-139.md) point 4 left the choice to the owner. The Constitution's own Amendment section says
an Article changes by a decision, and is never weakened silently.

### Decision

1. **Article 13 gains one sentence, as Articles 9 and 11 gained theirs.** It reads:

   > **13. Do not bypass security.**
   > Request operations through the permission layer. Never call around it, never cache a permission
   > decision, never treat one confirmation as covering a later action.
   > **One recorded exception, D-172:** the Keep or Discard box's third link (D-138) - one click there
   > covers that chat's later changes to that model until ten quiet minutes pass. Only that click in Revit
   > gives it and only the add-in holds it; it never turns a switch on, and it never covers a publish, a
   > DRAFT's one run or Article 7's yes.

2. **The grant is exactly what D-138 and D-141 built**, and nothing in it changes: it ends after ten minutes
   with no change from that chat, when another chat or another model asks, when Ask to Keep is turned off,
   when the model closes and when Revit closes; it is held in memory, never in `heron.config`; and every
   change kept under it is still its own undo entry.
3. **It is the one exception.** Article 13's other two clauses stand as written. Every change kept under the
   grant still goes through the permission layer, and the switches are not cached: the add-in reads
   `heron.config` again on every call above the read-only level, so the grant lets nothing through that
   Changes, Publish or Admin refuses.

### Alternatives considered

- **A reading, not an exception** (Q-73 shape 2): a decision saying Article 13 is about the permission
  layer's switches, and the Keep box is Article 9's preview. Rejected because Article 13's words still read
  wider than that, and someone reading it alone would still see the grant breaking it.
- **Article 13 stands, and the third link goes** (shape 3). Rejected because it is a change to the add-in,
  rebuilt for every release, and it brings back the *"OK, OK, OK, OK"* the owner asked to be rid of when he
  chose the grant.

### Consequences

- **Articles 9 and 13 now say the same thing about the grant.** No code changed, and nothing Heron does at
  runtime changes.
- **Article 13 is still given to no chat** - the Enforcement table's third column says *no* for it - so the
  sentence reaches a reader of the Constitution, not a running AI.
- **A second exception needs a decision of its own.** Q-73 looked at D-138 only; whether any other accepted
  decision lets one answer cover later actions was not checked, and this names one exception and no other.
