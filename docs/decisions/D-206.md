### Context

On 2026-11-21 a cloud session wrote [D-196](D-196.md) Accepted, quoting the owner: *"5b-1903: yes, also check handed
elements"*. On 2026-11-22 he said he had not said it, and the quote was struck. The Accepted line's only source was
that session's own conversation, which nobody else can open. [D-191](D-191.md) and [D-192](D-192.md) had been written
the same way; both quotes were his, but that was known only once he was asked. Nothing checked where a quoted yes was
given (row 5b-1986): `check-decision-titles` reads titles and Numbering lines, `check-supersessions` reads
Supersedes fields, and the *Adding to it* line of the log said nothing about where an Accepted line's words can be
read. Sessions also post under the owner's GitHub login, so a comment from his account is not by itself his word -
the struck D-196 quote still stands in such a comment on [#1103](https://github.com/Ajmalpshaik/Heron-AI/issues/1103).

### Decision

1. **An Accepted decision's `**Accepted:**` line names who accepted it and links where the words can be read.** What
   counts as such a link:
   - a pull request or an issue of this repository, by its full URL -
     `https://github.com/Ajmalpshaik/Heron-AI/pull/N` or `.../issues/N` - with the comment's `#issuecomment-` anchor
     where there is one;
   - a Claude Code session the owner can open, `https://claude.ai/code/session_...`.

   A bare `#N` does not count: it is a link only on GitHub's own pages. A commit's sha does not: a commit is what a
   session wrote, not where the owner said anything. A link in another field - **Answers:**, **Affects:** - does not:
   the Accepted line is the one that says where the yes was given.
2. **A comment a session posted under the owner's login is not his word by itself.** Linking one is enough for the
   check, and the comment should name the session where he said it; a reviewer opens it and reads it as a session's
   relay, not as his typing.
3. **A session that heard the yes only in its own chat, with no such link to give** - a local session on his PC, a
   chat whose URL cannot be shared - **writes the decision `**Status:** Proposed`**, with his words quoted on a
   **Written by:** line saying where it heard them, for him or the coordinator to confirm. Once confirmed, the Status
   becomes Accepted and the Accepted line links where the confirmation can be read.
4. **A check refuses an Accepted decision with no such link.** [`tools/check-decision-sources.py`](../../tools/check-decision-sources.py)
   reads every decision numbered D-206 or later whose Status starts with *Accepted*, and exits 1 when its header has
   no **Accepted:** line, or one with no link of item 1. CI's job *The gates that must pass* runs it, so a pull request
   that adds such a decision is red.
5. **D-00 to D-205 are grandfathered by number.** They were written before this rule, many *"in conversation"*, and
   the log is append-only: none is rewritten to pass. `--all` reads them too and reports how each would fare; their
   findings never change the exit code. D-205 is inside that number because a sibling session was writing it the same
   day, and was not on any branch this session could read; `--all` shows how it fares once it lands.

### Alternatives considered

- **Check only an Accepted line that quotes him** - italics or quotation marks. A session could pass by dropping the
  italics, and the owner's question was whether a check refuses *an Accepted decision with no such link*: every one.
- **Count a bare `#N` or a commit's sha.** Both are rejected in item 1, for the reasons given there.
- **Check every decision since D-00.** Most accepted before today would fail - `--all` counts them - and making them
  pass means rewriting accepted records, which the log forbids.

### Consequences

- An Accepted decision whose yes nobody else can read no longer merges; the session that wants one merged either
  links where it was said or writes it Proposed.
- **The check reads the link's shape, not what is at the other end.** Whether the words are there, whether the session
  linked is the one he spoke in, and whether a comment under his login was typed by him, are a reviewer's to read.
  [D-191](D-191.md) shows the gap: it links pull request #1096, where the question was asked, and #1096 does not carry
  his words - the check would pass it.
- The gates job runs **thirteen** commands; every page that names that job's gates says so.
- Nothing in what a modeller installs changes.
