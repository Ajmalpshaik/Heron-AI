## F43 — the fire protection design engine leaves five things only the owner can settle

### 🟠 F43. The fire protection design engine leaves five decisions for the owner - the table line, his own file against NFPA, the tables not held, a hydraulic fragment, and sprinklers above and below a ceiling

Raised 2026-10-02 with [`HERON-MEP-FPD-002`](../../brain/heron_fire.py), the fire protection design
engine built on the owner's request and described in [42](../42-fire-protection-design.md). It is built
and tested for arithmetic; these five are decisions, not gaps, and [42 §15](../42-fire-protection-design.md)
carries the reasoning for each.

1. **A table the modeller asks about is answered as that table.** Every design criterion a calculation
   needs - a density, a spacing limit, a C factor - is asked, [D-33](../decisions/D-33.md) as the HVAC
   engine keeps it. But where the question IS a table's - *"what does the light hazard schedule give for
   ten sprinklers"*, *"how high may the deflector sit at 700 mm from the beam"* - the engine answers from
   that table, labelled as the table's figure ([42 §3](../42-fire-protection-design.md)). Keep that
   line, or ask for every figure, the schedule's too?
2. **Your own firefighting file and NFPA disagree in six places** ([42 §12](../42-fire-protection-design.md)
   rows 1 to 6): kitchens as ordinary hazard group 2 where the annex lists restaurant service areas
   under group 1; a standpipe system's most flow as 1250 gpm where NFPA 14 gives 1000 gpm for a
   building sprinklered throughout; 450 mm below a deflector to storage, against a figure not read
   here; 23 m of extinguisher travel against NFPA 10's 22.9 m; a jockey pump at about 1 % of the main
   pump against NFPA 20's annex; manual call points at 30 m against NFPA 72's 61 m. Which governs the
   office's work - one answer for each, as D-110 settled the neck velocity?
3. **The tables that could not be checked are not held** ([42 §10](../42-fire-protection-design.md)) -
   fittings' equivalent lengths, the C-factor table, the K-factor list, the temperature ratings, the
   minimum operating pressure, sidewall and extended-coverage spacing, the design area adjustments, the
   beam rule past 4 ft, five pipe schedule cells, and the 1.2 in 1.2 sqrt A, which is used but
   recalled. Read them from your copy of NFPA 13 into the engine, or leave them asked?
4. **A hydraulic calculation that reads the pipe network from Revit itself.** Today the host reads the
   network and hands it to `hydraulic`. A fragment would do it in one call and carry the same
   arithmetic in C# - a second home for one fact.
5. **Sprinklers above and below a ceiling are asked every time** whether they combine in one design area
   or are calculated apart, the larger governing. Is there an office practice to record once?

**None of the five is answered yet.**
