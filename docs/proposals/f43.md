## F43 — the fire protection design engine leaves six things only the owner can settle

### 🟠 F43. The fire protection design engine leaves six decisions for the owner - the table line, his own file against NFPA, the tables not held, a hydraulic fragment, sprinklers above and below a ceiling, and QCDD's own tables

Raised 2026-10-02 with [`HERON-MEP-FPD-002`](../../brain/heron_fire.py), the fire protection design
engine built on the owner's request and described in [42](../42-fire-protection-design.md). It is built
and tested for arithmetic; these six are decisions, not gaps, and [42 §15](../42-fire-protection-design.md)
carries the reasoning for each. Item 6 was added with the second round of research the same day.

1. **A table the modeller asks about is answered as that table.** Every design criterion a calculation
   needs - a density, a spacing limit, a C factor - is asked, [D-33](../decisions/D-33.md) as the HVAC
   engine keeps it. But where the question IS a table's - *"what does the light hazard schedule give for
   ten sprinklers"*, *"how high may the deflector sit at 700 mm from the beam"* - the engine answers from
   that table, labelled as the table's figure ([42 §3](../42-fire-protection-design.md)). Keep that
   line, or ask for every figure, the schedule's too?
2. **Your own firefighting file and NFPA disagree in six places** ([42 §12](../42-fire-protection-design.md)
   rows 1 to 6): kitchens as ordinary hazard group 2 where the annex lists restaurant service areas
   under group 1; a standpipe system's most flow as 1250 gpm where NFPA 14 gives 1000 gpm for a
   building sprinklered throughout; 450 mm below a deflector to storage, against NFPA 13's 18 in
   (457 mm) - read in round two; 23 m of extinguisher travel against NFPA 10's 22.9 m; a jockey pump at
   about 1 % of the main pump against NFPA 20's annex; manual call points at 30 m against NFPA 72's
   61 m - BS 5839-1's own straight-line figure, round two found, beside its 45 m along the route.
   Which governs the office's work - one answer for each, as D-110 settled the neck velocity?
3. **The tables that could not be checked are not held** ([42 §10](../42-fire-protection-design.md)).
   Round two filled the C-factor table, the K-factor list, the temperature ratings, the minimum
   operating pressure, the design area adjustments, light hazard's combustible rows and the beam rule to
   5 ft 6 in, and confirmed 1.2 sqrt A, all through search extracts. Still not held: most of the
   equivalent length chart, five pipe schedule cells, the sidewall areas, the beam rule past 5 ft 6 in,
   and the EN 12845 and FM Global cells §10 names. Read them from your copies into the engine, or leave
   them asked?
4. **A hydraulic calculation that reads the pipe network from Revit itself.** Today the host reads the
   network and hands it to `hydraulic`. A fragment would do it in one call and carry the same
   arithmetic in C# - a second home for one fact.
5. **Sprinklers above and below a ceiling are asked every time** whether they combine in one design area
   or are calculated apart, the larger governing. Is there an office practice to record once?
6. **QCDD's own tables** ([42 §11](../42-fire-protection-design.md)). The hose reels, the tank's
   compartments and refill, the standpipe threshold and the storage figures came through search
   extracts of the Technical Requirements Guide and the 2015 annex - their flow and pressure not found,
   the storage rows and the 28 m boundary disagreeing between copies. Can the office give Heron a copy,
   so they are read rather than reported?

**None of the six is answered yet.**
