## 3. What transfers

### 3.1 ✅ TAKEN — contextual retrieval, and Heron gets most of it for free

**The technique.** Anthropic's [Contextual Retrieval](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/contextual-retrieval)
observes that a chunk is embedded **in isolation**, having lost all knowledge of where it came from. The
fix is to prepend a short piece of context situating the chunk in its document, *then* embed and index
it. Reported: **retrieval failures down 49%, and 67% when combined with re-ranking.**

**The cost, and why it collides with a Heron decision.** In their form that context is **generated by a
model, once per chunk, at index time**. That is a per-chunk model call — and
[D-24](../../DECISIONS.md) chose local embeddings precisely because *"a per-call cost makes rebuilding
the index something to avoid, and an index nobody rebuilds quietly stops matching what is on disk."*
Adopting it as written would re-introduce the exact cost D-24 removed.

> ### **And for Heron's corpus, the model is not needed.**
>
> The context that situates a clause **is its heading path.** *QCS 2014 → Section 21 Mechanical →
> 21.3 Ductwork → 21.3.2 Insulation.* That is not generated — it is **read off the document's own
> structure**, which [§3.3](../plans/rag/00-structure.md) already decided to store as a parent link.
>
> **So Heron can have the mechanism deterministically, offline, at zero marginal cost**, for exactly
> the class of document it cares most about: standards, specifications and authority requirements,
> which are numbered and nested by law and habit.

**It does not cover everything.** A meeting note or an unstructured project PDF has no heading path, and
for those the technique needs the model or nothing. **That is a smaller problem than it looks** — the
documents where a citation must be exact are the structured ones.

**In Revit terms:** a detail with no sheet number on it. The detail is correct and nobody can find it or
say where it came from. The sheet number is not extra information; it is what makes the detail usable.

**Taken as R-66 and R-67.**

### 3.2 ✅ TAKEN — the failure mode that would matter most in Qatar MEP work

From the chunking research, stated plainly and worth quoting because it names a defect rather than a
technique:

> *"compliance teams can receive answers that leave out exception clauses because the chunk containing
> that exception was split right at the paragraph boundary where the general rule ended and the
> qualification began."*

**This is the worst thing this system could do**, and it is worse than a miss. A standard reads *"ducts
shall be insulated … except where installed within conditioned spaces."* Split between those halves,
retrieve the first, and Heron states the opposite of the requirement **with a citation attached**, which
makes it more convincing than an uncited guess.

It is not hypothetical for the work this is being built for: QCS, Ashghal requirements and every
specification a consultant answers to are written as *rule, then qualification*.

**So a chunk is never split between a rule and its exception.** Structural splitting is most of the
answer; the rest is that a split point immediately before *except*, *unless*, *provided that*,
*save that* or *however* is **not a split point**. Deterministic, checkable, and testable with one
paragraph.

**Taken as R-68.**

---

### 3.3 ✅ TAKEN — a person looks at the chunking before it is trusted

RAGFlow's chunking is *"intelligent and explainable"*, with **visual representation of the chunking and
an opportunity for human review**. That is the part worth taking, and it is not a UI idea — it is the
same principle this repository already runs on.

[D-30](../../DECISIONS.md): the machine gathers the evidence and **a person signs**. A fragment is not
`PROVEN` because a tool said so. **Chunking deserves the same treatment**, because every later stage —
retrieval, citation, the fabrication check — inherits its mistakes silently. A badly chunked standard
does not fail; it answers, confidently, forever.

**Taken as R-69.** It does not need a UI: the ingester printing the chunk boundaries of one document,
for a person to read once, is the whole requirement.

### 3.4 ⏸ OWNER'S CALL — Docling, and the biggest dependency question this project has faced

[Docling](https://docling.org/) (IBM Research) does what Stage 1 needs and does it **locally**: PDF,
Word, PowerPoint, HTML and images into structured output that **preserves headings, tables, lists and
reading order**, with chunking on **structural nodes rather than character counts, keeping the header
hierarchy** — which is §3.3 and §3.1 above, already built. It has a permissive licence, installs with
`pip`, and the documents never leave the machine.

**And it is by far the largest dependency this repository would have taken.**

| | Today | With Docling |
|---|---|---|
| What `brain/` needs | **`pyyaml`.** That is the whole list | `pyyaml`, plus a document-parsing stack with deep-learning models for layout and tables |
| First run | works | **downloads models to a local cache** — every call after that is offline, but the first needs a network |
| Python | as installed | **3.10 or newer** |

**Both halves are real.** Writing a PDF parser that recovers clause numbering from a real QCS PDF is not
a small job, and doing it badly poisons everything downstream (§3.2). Equally, `mcp/client/` is barred
from dependencies entirely because it must run on a locked-down machine, `brain/` is allowed them but
has taken exactly one, and *"install this and it downloads models"* is a different promise from
[D-01](../../DECISIONS.md)'s.

**Not decided here. It is the owner's, and it is worth ten minutes of his attention rather than a
default.** Recorded as **S-4** in [`03-working-note.md`](../plans/rag/03-working-note.md).

## 4. What does not transfer

### 4.1 ❌ LightRAG's graph — twice over

**First, it needs a model to build.** Its nodes and edges come from **LLM-powered entity-relation
extraction over every chunk** at index time. `brain/` calls no model, [D-24](../../DECISIONS.md) exists
to keep indexing free, and re-indexing must stay something Heron does rather than avoids.

**Second, and decisively, this repository has already measured the shape.**
[`34 §2.13`](../../34-patterns-adapted.md) ran a graph retrieval stream at **six settings and all six
lost**, with the deciding property named: **density**. Heron's graph runs a median of 50 neighbours per
fragment, worst 230.

**One honest qualification.** LightRAG's graph is **entity-based** — *this clause mentions insulation* —
not composition-based like Heron's, so it is not the same graph and its density would differ. That is
exactly the position [`00-structure.md` §3.2](../plans/rag/00-structure.md) already reached this
morning: **conditional, gated on a neighbour count.** LightRAG does not change that gate; it is another
reason to keep it.

### 4.2 ❌ RAGFlow's stack

Elasticsearch or Infinity, Redis, MySQL, MinIO, Docker, 16 GB RAM, 50 GB disk, and *"cannot operate as a
standalone, serverless application"*. [D-23](../../DECISIONS.md) rejected this class on the install
constraint before any of it was read. **The template-based chunking and the reviewable chunk boundaries
are the parts worth having, and both are in §3.2 and §3.3 above without any of the stack.**

### 4.3 ❌ RAGAS, ARES and the LLM-as-judge evaluation frameworks

The standard tools decompose an answer into claims and score each **with a model**. Two problems, and
the second is the one that decides it:

1. **They need a model, per evaluation.** Heron's gates run offline with no keys, on a container with no
   network.
2. **Correlation between RAGAS metrics and human evaluation is reported at 0.55.** A judge that agrees
   with a person barely more than half the time is not a gate; it is a second opinion with a number
   attached, and this repository has spent months learning what a confident number does to a document.

**Heron is already doing the deterministic thing and should keep doing it.**
[`tools/check-routing.py`](../../../tools/check-routing.py) asks every fragment's own declared sentences
back to the search and reports #1 and top-3 — **precision@1 and recall@3, exactly, with no judge** — and
[`tools/check-intrusion.py`](../../../tools/check-intrusion.py) asks the opposite question. Together with
the fabrication check taken this morning, that is a **fully deterministic evaluation stack**, which the
frameworks above cannot offer at any price.

**This is the second time today the outside reading has argued for something Heron already had.**

## 5. The number worth writing down

`sqlite-vec` does brute-force exact search, and the field's rule of thumb is that this is **fast enough
below roughly 500,000 vectors**; past that its approximate index is weaker than a purpose-built store's.

Heron is at **360** vectors — one per fragment. **The ceiling is nowhere near, and it is not infinite**,
and the thing that will move it is documents: a chunked standards library is tens of thousands of
chunks, and a large company knowledge base could reach six figures.

**So D-23 holds, and now has a stated limit rather than an implied one.** Taken as **R-70**: the vector
count is reported, so the day it approaches that ceiling is a day somebody notices rather than a day
retrieval quietly gets slow.
